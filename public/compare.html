Audio captcha, the path blind users depend on, is the cheapest row on the menu: €0.45 per 1,000. Free browser add-ons like Buster exploit the same audio path with speech recognition at no cost at all.

Why they cannot be fixed

Every type above produces a token: solve once, hand the token over, done. A token is portable, so a farm of paid clickers or a vision model can mint it anywhere and replay it anywhere. Harder puzzles only raise the farm's price by fractions of a cent. That is the whole market's failure mode: they test knowledge or perception, both of which are for sale.

What we do instead

1. Presence, not puzzles. Our checks must be performed live: follow a target that teleports at random moments, click flashes inside a sub-second window, tap a beat. The server stamps each challenge's clock and rejects any answer that did not take real time. There is no token to sell because the performance itself is the proof.

2. Nothing is replayable or pre-trainable. Every session gets fresh random paths, dart times, flash gaps and words, signed and single-use. A captured answer is worthless twice.

3. A voice path with dignity. Disabled users speak four words at their own pace. Untimed, scored on vocal timing, not speed.

4. A fair-share ledger behind the door. No check is unbreakable forever, so a passed check buys a capped allowance, not unlimited access. Beating the captcha earns $2 of API calls, not the keys to the site. Honest AI agents skip the puzzle entirely: they declare with a signed key and get their own budget.

Honest scope: we do not claim these checks can never be beaten. We claim they cannot be beaten cheaply, at scale, by an API call today - and that when they are beaten, the ledger bounds what that buys.
